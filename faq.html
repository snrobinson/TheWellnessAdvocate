FAQ

Questions, answered plainly

The questions prospective patients ask most about Direct Primary Care, hormone health, and psychiatry at The Wellness Advocate.

What is Direct Primary Care, and how is it different from a regular doctor’s office?

Direct Primary Care (DPC) replaces per-visit insurance billing with a flat monthly membership paid directly to the practice. Because the practice doesn’t bill insurance for your primary care, visits can be longer, easier to schedule, and focused entirely on you. You always know the cost up front.

Do you accept insurance?

No — The Wellness Advocate is a cash-pay practice and does not bill insurance for its services. That is a deliberate choice: it keeps pricing transparent and keeps care decisions between you and your provider. You may still use your insurance for prescriptions, labs, imaging, specialists, and hospital care outside the practice. Do I still need health insurance if I become a member?

Membership is not insurance and doesn’t replace it. Most members keep at least a high-deductible or catastrophic plan for hospitalizations, emergencies, and specialist care. A licensed insurance agent can help you decide what coverage makes sense alongside membership.

What is BHRT, and how do I know if it’s right for me?

Bioidentical hormone replacement therapy (BHRT) uses hormones structurally identical to those your body produces to address symptoms associated with hormonal change, such as those related to perimenopause, menopause, or low testosterone. Whether it’s appropriate for you depends on your symptoms, labs, history, and preferences — that’s what the consult process is for. It starts with lab work and an in-depth review, and therapy is only started if you and your provider decide together that it makes sense. Read more about the BHRT consult process.
